# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be straightforward and be decent. Technical disagreement is the point of this project; contempt is
not.

## Scope

This applies to every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code
review, and any private correspondence that follows from them. It also applies when you are
representing the project publicly.

## Expected behavior

- **Argue about the work, not the person.** "This breaks the ownership rule at reset" is useful.
  "This is amateur hour" is not.
- **Show your reasoning.** Assertions about wire behavior, ownership, or portability should point at
  the specification, a test, a failing input, or a measurement.
- **Assume competence and good faith.** Contributors arrive with different context. A question that
  seems obvious to you may be the first time someone has seen a generational object ID.
- **Accept a decision once it is made**, or escalate it in the open with new evidence. Re-litigating
  a settled point in every thread is a form of noise.
- **Be patient about response times.** This is maintained on a best-effort basis.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained personal hostility, in public or in private.
- Demeaning comments about who someone is, rather than what they wrote — including about ancestry,
  nationality, disability, appearance, body size, age, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual
  orientation, religion, or level of experience.
- Sexualized language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, or persistent bad-faith argument after being asked to stop.
- Using the project to distribute malware, exfiltrate data, or attack third parties. Note that
  security research is explicitly welcome under [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md); attacking people is not.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately to **info@microperceptron.com**. Reports are read by the project
maintainers.

Please include what happened, where, roughly when, and any links. You do not need to prove your
case, and you do not need to be the target of the behavior to report it.

We will acknowledge your report and tell you what we intend to do. We will not share your identity
with the person reported without asking you first. If a report concerns a maintainer, say so, and it
will be handled by someone else.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity and history, maintainers may respond with a private correction, a public
correction, a temporary block from project spaces, or a permanent ban. We will say which one we are
applying and why.

If you believe an enforcement decision was wrong, reply to the same address with your reasoning. We
would rather revisit a decision than defend a bad one.
